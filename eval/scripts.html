rounded syllabary
Syllable chart A syllabary in the rounded hand: each syllable has its own sign. A consonant with no vowel after it is written small, as the syllable with u, with a short tail. Its scribes' habits: letters joined along the baseline.

capitals abugida
Consonants An abugida in the capital-letter hand: each consonant letter carries the vowel a. Its scribes' habits: wedge-shaped stroke heads, corners softened into curves, narrow letters.tangaʻanafamakalahawapa
Vowel signs Other vowels are signs attached to the consonant: above, below, before or after it. A slanted stroke under a letter cancels the vowel.tatetitutot (no vowel)
Independent vowels At the start of a word or after another vowel, vowels have full letters.aeiuo
runic syllabary
Syllable chart A syllabary in the carved runic hand: each consonant has one base shape, and the vowel is a mark attached to it in a fixed place (the bare shape is the syllable with a), as in Ge'ez. A consonant with no vowel after it is written small, as the syllable with u, with a short tail. Its scribes' habits: a backward slant, narrow letters, hooked stroke endings, a head line joining the letters of a word.
